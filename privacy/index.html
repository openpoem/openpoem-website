OpenPoem® Privacy & AI

What we send, and what we keep.

OpenPoem reads what lives between the words. To do that, the text you give it has to be read — and you should know by whom, where, and what is left behind afterwards.

In one paragraph. The text you ask OpenPoem to read travels to Anthropic in the United States, where Claude reads it and sends back a reading. We keep none of it: not the text, not the reading. Recording and transcription never leave your iPhone at all. What we do keep is a count — an anonymous device identifier and how many readings it asked for — so five a day can be free without being drained by a script.

Which AI reads, and where

OpenPoem is built on Claude, a model made by Anthropic, and every reading is written by it. Anthropic's servers are in the United States, so your text crosses the Atlantic over an encrypted connection and is processed there under Anthropic's API terms and privacy policy. Their retention and handling are theirs, not ours; we link rather than paraphrase, because we cannot promise on their behalf.

Everything the app writes is written by an AI. The reading, the advice, the moves it names, the answers in keep thinking — all of it. It is a reading, not a fact, and it can be wrong.

What stays on your iPhone

  • Recordings and transcription. Speech is turned into text by Apple's on-device engine. The audio never leaves the phone; only the text you choose to have read does.
  • Your library. Transcripts and their readings live in the app on your device, and in your own encrypted device backup if you make one. Delete an item and it is gone — there is no copy elsewhere.
  • Your own API key, if you add one, is stored in the iPhone Keychain and sent only to Anthropic.

What we keep on our side

Only what is needed to give away five readings a day without being abused, and to see when someone tries:

  • an anonymous device identifier generated on your phone (no name, no email, no account);
  • Apple's vendor identifier for this app on this device;
  • counts and timestamps: how many readings, first and last use;
  • for refused requests: the reason and a hashed IP address — never the address itself.

No text is ever stored. Not the text you send, not the reading that comes back, not in a database and not in a log. The tables have no column for it.

Who reads what, and about whom

A reading is always about the text, never about a person. The app names what a text does — where it points, where it judges, which rhetorical moves it makes, what runs under it — and it is instructed, in every request, never to diagnose, score or judge the human being who wrote it. Marks of evasion are marks in language; they are not proof that anyone lied.

If you paste something written by someone else, you are responsible for what you share. For private use that is ordinary and fine. If you use OpenPoem at work — on colleagues, candidates or clients — you take on the obligations that come with it, and OpenPoem may not be used to assess, select or monitor people.

Verification, and why

Free readings run on our own API key, so the app asks Apple to confirm that the request comes from a real iPhone running OpenPoem (DeviceCheck). It proves the device, not you: no identity is involved, and nothing about you is sent to Apple.

Why we are allowed to do this

Two grounds under the GDPR, and no consent banner, because consent is not the right instrument here:

  • To do what you asked (Art. 6(1)(b)): sending your text to be read is the service itself — you press Read, it goes.
  • A legitimate interest (Art. 6(1)(f)) in keeping the free tier alive: the anonymous device id, the daily counts, the device verification and the hashed IP on a refused request exist to stop one script draining what is meant for many. Nothing about it profiles you, and none of it is used for anything else.

The transfer to Anthropic in the United States rests on the safeguards in Anthropic's data processing agreement, including the European Commission's standard contractual clauses. See their privacy centre for what they do with API data.

How long we keep it

  • Your text and its reading: never. Not for a minute — there is no column in our database that could hold them.
  • Daily counts (which device read how often): deleted after 30 days.
  • Refused requests (reason, hashed IP): deleted after 90 days.
  • A device record: deleted after a year without a reading.

This runs by itself, every night — it is a job in the database, not a good intention.

Your choices

  • Erase everything — Settings → privacy & ai. One tap deletes every transcript and reading on the phone, your key, and the rows we hold on the server. There is no way back, and no copy elsewhere.
  • Forget this device — a new anonymous id starts and the counts we hold stop pointing at this phone.
  • Delete a single transcript in the app, or delete the app to remove everything on the device.
  • Bring your own Anthropic key and the free tier is bypassed entirely — readings then go straight from your phone to Anthropic, billed to you.
  • Write to info@openpoem.org to ask what is held under your device identifier, to have it corrected, or to object to the processing above. We answer within a month.
  • Unhappy with how we handle it? You can complain to the Dutch data protection authority, the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens, or to the authority where you live.
